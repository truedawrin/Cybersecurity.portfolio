## 📌 Overview

A career exploration room that maps out the different roles available in cybersecurity. This room is not a hands-on hacking challenge — it's a guided tour of the industry landscape, helping beginners understand what each role does, what skills it requires, and which TryHackMe learning paths lead to it. The room concludes with a quiz to help identify which role suits your personality and interests best.

## 🎯 Objectives

- Understand the breadth of career opportunities in cybersecurity.
- Learn the day-to-day responsibilities of each major role.
- Identify the skills, tools, and learning paths required for each role.
- Use the career quiz to reflect on personal strengths and interests.

### 🔍 Enumeration

This room enumerates the major cybersecurity roles, their responsibilities, required skills, and recommended learning paths.

**1. Security Analyst**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                    |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Monitor networks, analyze threats, and respond to incidents. They evaluate company networks to uncover actionable data and recommendations for engineers. |
| **Skills needed**    | SIEM tools, log analysis, basic scripting, communication.                                                                                                 |
| **Recommended Path** | Pre Security, SOC Level 1                                                                                                                                 |
| **Personal Insight** | This is the "first responder" of cyber — ideal for those who enjoy puzzles and real-time problem-solving.                                                 |

**2. Security Engineer**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                   |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Build and maintain secure systems, implement firewalls, and harden infrastructure. They use threat and vulnerability data to develop security solutions. |
| **Skills needed**    | Networking, system administration, scripting, cloud security.                                                                                            |
| **Recommended Path** | Cyber Defense, Red Teaming                                                                                                                               |
| **Personal Insight** | This role appeals to builders — those who want to proactively design secure environments rather than just react to threats.                              |

**3. Incident Responder**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                              |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **What they do**     | Investigate breaches, contain threats, and perform forensics. They create plans, policies, and protocols for organizations to enact during and following incidents. |
| **Skills needed**    | Digital forensics, malware analysis, threat intelligence.                                                                                                           |
| **Recommended Path** | SOC Level 1, Threat Hunting                                                                                                                                         |
| **Personal Insight** | This is the cyber equivalent of a detective. If you love digging into logs and uncovering what went wrong, this might be your path.                                 |

**4. Digital Forensics Examiner**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                                |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Collect, preserve, and analyze digital evidence. In law enforcement, this helps solve crimes; in corporate security, it investigates policy violations and incidents. |
| **Skills needed**    | Forensic tools, chain of custody, disk/memory analysis.                                                                                                               |
| **Recommended Path** | SOC Level 1, DFIR modules                                                                                                                                             |
| **Personal Insight** | Perfect for those who like to "play detective" and are detail-oriented.                                                                                               |

**5. Malware Analyst**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                            |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Analyze suspicious programs to discover what they do and write reports about their findings. Sometimes called a reverse-engineer. |
| **Skills needed**    | Strong programming background (especially assembly and C), low-level analysis.                                                    |
| **Recommended Path** | Reverse Engineering, Malware Analysis modules                                                                                     |
| **Personal Insight** | Requires deep technical curiosity and patience. It's about understanding the "how" and "why" of malicious code.                   |

**6. Penetration Tester (My Focus)**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| :------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Test the security of systems and software by attempting to uncover flaws and vulnerabilities through systemized hacking. They exploit these vulnerabilities to evaluate risk and help companies rectify issues. |
| **Skills needed**    | Kali Linux, Metasploit, Burp Suite, scripting (Python/Bash), report writing, strong ethics.                                                                                                                     |
| **Recommended Path** | **Jr Penetration Tester**, **Offensive Pentesting**, **Red Teaming**                                                                                                                                            |
| **Personal Insight** | This is the most "Hollywood" role — fun, technical, and high-impact. It requires strong ethics and communication skills, not just technical prowess.                                                            |

**7. Red Teamer**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **What they do**     | Share similarities with penetration testers, but with a more targeted job role. They imitate cyber criminals' actions, emulate malicious attacks, retain access, and avoid detection. |
| **Skills needed**    | Advanced exploitation, social engineering, operational security (OpSec), persistence techniques.                                                                                      |
| **Recommended Path** | Red Teaming, Advanced Offensive Security                                                                                                                                              |
| **Personal Insight** | This is the "adversary simulation" role — a step beyond pentesting, focusing on stealth and long-term access.                                                                         |

**8. Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)**

| Aspect               | Detail                                                                                                               |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What they do**     | Ensure organizations meet security standards and regulations. Align security with business goals.                    |
| **Skills needed**    | Policy writing, risk assessment, frameworks (ISO 27001, NIST), communication.                                        |
| **Recommended Path** | Security Principles, GRC modules                                                                                     |
| **Personal Insight** | Often overlooked but critical. Perfect for those with a strategic mindset who understand both security and business. |

## 🛡️ Defensive Perspective

Understanding the full career landscape is itself a defensive skill — it helps you know who to collaborate with. A penetration tester works closely with defenders (Security Analysts, Incident Responders) to ensure findings are remediated. Knowing the roles also helps in a real engagement: you need to know who owns the system you're testing, who will receive your report, and who will implement the fixes.

## 🧠 What I Learned

- Cybersecurity is broad — there's a role for every personality type (technical, analytical, strategic).
- **Soft skills matter:** Communication, documentation, and collaboration are just as important as technical prowess.
- The difference between a Penetration Tester (project-based, scoped, report-driven) and a Red Teamer (adversary simulation, stealth-focused, long-term access).
- My chosen path is **Penetration Testing**, and the relevant TryHackMe paths are **Jr Penetration Tester**, **Offensive Pentesting**, and **Red Teaming**.

## 📚 Key Takeaways

- Start with curiosity. Pick a path that excites you and build from there.
- The **Jr Penetration Tester** path is my immediate next milestone after completing Pre Security.
- A portfolio of practical projects can substitute for formal certifications in the eyes of many employers.
- The industry rewards continuous learning — there is always a next specialization (web app security, exploit development, red teaming).
